## What the prototype found (2026-10-29, main session, M2 Pro, headed Chromium on Metal, dpr 2)

- **`TILE_LIFT` is not an anti-fighting offset**, though it places one of the two surfaces: it lifts
  the Kit's tiles, whose root node sits 0.01 low, so their grass lies at y = 0. The offset is
  `GROUND_Y = −0.003`: the ground plane under the tiles, which would otherwise fight that grass.
- **The canvas's depth, measured.** `gl.getParameter(gl.DEPTH_BITS)` says 24 — the format Chrome
  asks for — and `EXT_clip_control` is present; three 0.186 takes `reversedDepthBuffer: true`
  (the depth function becomes `GEQUAL`). A bare three.js page, one camera 0.45 above two 40-tile
  planes looking 6 ahead, the lower plane drawn last so it wins every tie, counted the pixels where
  it shows through (1280 × 760, 537,938 plane pixels):

  | offset | standard, near 0.02 | reversed, near 0.02 | standard, near 0.1 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0.0003 and above | 0 | 0 | 0 |
  | 0.0001 | 3,145 | **0** | 721 |
  | 0.00003 | 45,604 | 5,153 | 372 |
  | 0.00001 | 30,094 | 7,980 | 19,470 |
  | 0.000001 | 127,808 | 16,663 | 66,065 |

  On a 24-bit fixed-point buffer reversing the mapping changes nothing (the steps stay uniform in
  depth); here it removed the fight at 1e-4 and cut it ninefold at 3e-5 — so on the M2 the canvas
  stores float depth (ANGLE's Metal backend; Apple GPUs have no 24-bit unorm depth format), as the
  review argued. Raising near fivefold helps too, as the step's 1/near says. Neither removes the
  offset: coplanar surfaces fight under any precision.
- **A first probe was wrong and is withdrawn.** It painted the race's ground magenta and counted it
  in the countdown's frame; the extra magenta it read as a fight at an offset of 0.0001 was the
  camera standing a little differently from run to run — every one of its frames, offset 0
  included, shows the grass verge intact.
- **The margin today.** The ring is 10 × 9 tiles; the farthest tile either camera sees is 14.8
  tiles away (the overview stands 7 up and 7.5 back from the centre; the chase camera, 1.1 behind
  the car, is bounded at 14.6). There `Δz` = 6.5 × 10⁻⁴: the offset of 3 × 10⁻³ has 4.6 times the
  step. (The planning estimate was 15 tiles and 6.7 × 10⁻⁴; the test computes it.) Nothing
  flickers, so nothing changes. Reversed depth would help on the M2 but is not switched on: no
  fight to remove, and shadows and the sky would have to be checked under it. It belongs to
  exercise 8, whose composer target gets a float depth texture on every platform.

## What the review found

An Opus review confirmed the step's formula (the window mapping's ½ cancels the 2 in 2fn; every
figure reproduces) and found:

1. **The test compared `|GROUND_Y|`, not the gap to the grass** — a ground above the grass, or
   `TILE_LIFT` moved so the grass sat level with it, stayed green. Fixed: a signed gap.
2. **The overview's distance depended on its offset's sign** (a mirrored offset of 20 computed 17.7
   tiles for a real 26). Fixed: the farthest of the four corners.
3. **"24-bit, so reversed depth buys nothing" was argued, not measured** — Apple GPUs have no
   24-bit depth format. The bare probe above measured it: reversed depth does help on the M2's
   canvas. The docs say so now; it still waits, for the reason in the margin above.
4. The probe's near-0.1 column had no failing row in the withdrawn probe; the bare probe has them.
5. The test took the rules' rectangle while the overview stands over the client's; identical today.
