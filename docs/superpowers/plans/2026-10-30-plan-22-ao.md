# Plan 22 — ambient occlusion in the race (the study's exercise 8): measured, not shipped

**Goal:** screen-space ambient occlusion for the race (RTR 11.3.6; proposal P5), the race's first
composer, P1's shared pipeline module and — since plan 20 — a float depth target with reversed
depth, if the meter says the look is worth its cost.

**Outcome:** the prototype ran, the meter and the pictures were read, and the answer is no: the
effect is hard to see in this scene and costs one to two and a half milliseconds. Nothing is
shipped; P1 keeps waiting for a second composer.

---

## What the prototype did (2026-10-30, main session, M2 Pro, headed Metal, vsync on)

A throwaway `RacePipeline` in the race's canvas: an `EffectComposer` into a half-float target with a
float `DepthTexture` (multisampled below dpr 2, as the arena's), `RenderPass`, three's `GTAOPass`
reading the scene's own depth and reconstructing normals from it (no second scene pass), and
`OutputPass` last; the renderer with `reversedDepthBuffer: true`. It drew correctly: the AO buffer
darkened under the cars, at the barriers' feet and along the kerbs; the composite was the race's
picture, shadows and the sky included, under reversed depth.

### Two traps in three 0.186

- **`GTAOPass` given a depth texture in its constructor throws** (`GTAOPass.js`): `setGBuffer` then
  reads `this.normalRenderTarget.depthTexture`, which only the no-texture branch creates. Construct
  it without parameters and call `setGBuffer(depthTexture)` afterwards. An unused half-float normal
  target stays allocated: memory, no draws.
- **`EffectComposer` draws the scene into its read buffer**, `renderTarget2`, a clone of the target
  you hand it — and the clone gets a depth texture of its own. Handed the original target's depth
  texture, GTAO read a depth texture the scene was never drawn into; the canvas came out blank, with
  "Framebuffer is incomplete: Attachments are not all the same size" in the console (its exact cause
  not traced). The depth to read is `composer.readBuffer.depthTexture`. It stays the right one only
  while the passes swap an even number of times a frame (RenderPass none, GTAO and OutputPass one
  each): with the AO pass disabled the read buffer alternates, and re-enabling it would read a
  frozen depth — take `readBuffer.depthTexture` again after any toggle.
- And for probing: StrictMode calls `useMemo` twice and keeps one result, so a debug handle set
  inside the memo pointed at the discarded composer (1 × 1, never sized). Set it in an effect.

### The cost (race, twenty players, GPU median)

| | dpr 1 | dpr 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Today, no composer (plan 21's after-runs, two each) | 0.89 / 0.88 ms | 2.63 / 2.60 ms |
| GTAO at full resolution, 16 + 16 samples (one run) | 4.00 ms | **22.32 ms** (p95 23.2: the 60 fps check fails) |
| GTAO at half resolution, 8 + 8 samples (one run) | 3.34 ms | 3.63 ms |
| …the same without the composer's multisampling (one run) | 2.10 ms | — (none at dpr 2) |
| The composer with multisampling, AO off (one run) | 1.42 ms | — |

Draw calls 17 → 22: GTAO's four draws and `OutputPass`; no second scene pass.

- **At dpr 2** AO's added cost was about 19.7 ms at full resolution and about 1.0 ms at half
  resolution with half the samples — two changes at once, so it does not isolate either. That full
  resolution added 6.4 times at dpr 2 what it added at dpr 1 (3.1 ms) for 4 times the pixels points
  at a tiled GPU storing and reloading full-screen targets between passes — a hypothesis, not
  measured.
- **At dpr 1** the same change only took the added cost from 3.1 to 2.5 ms. Reading the code (not
  measured): three resolves a multisampled target's colour and depth after every render into it;
  the composer's buffers are both 4× multisampled (a clone keeps `samples`); GTAO's default output
  draws a copy and a blend into the write buffer — two more full resolves a frame, at full
  resolution whatever the AO's own. The study doc recorded the same for the arena's bloom.
  0.88 + 0.54 (composer) + 1.22 (AO without multisampling) = 2.64 against 3.34 measured.

### The picture

One still frame (the countdown, the camera parked), AO blended in and out of the same render,
cropped 1:1 at dpr 2:

- defaults (radius 0.25, scale 1): a little darker behind the rear wheels; otherwise nothing.
- radius 0.5, scale 2: the red barrier block's foot a shade darker; cars unchanged to the eye.
- The AO buffer itself shows the contact clearly. The composite does not, for three reasons. The
  cars are already grounded by the sun's soft shadow maps, which give most of the contact P5 asked
  AO for. The asphalt under them is dark. The Kit's low-poly pieces have few creases.

## Decision

Not shipped (*decided by me*; the pictures were throwaway and are not kept, but the prototype above
is detailed enough to rebuild in an hour for the owner's eye). One to two and a half milliseconds
for a difference visible only in a 1:1 crop is the trade "optimise from a measurement" rules out.
What would change it: a scene lit mostly by the sky rather than the sun, denser geometry with
creases (buildings, stands, foliage), or a phone tier that needs the shadows gone and AO as a cheap
stand-in — each a new measurement, not this one.

With no AO, the race needs no composer, so P1's shared module still has one user and stays unbuilt,
and reversed float depth (plan 20) stays unneeded: nothing fights at today's offset.

## What the review found

An Opus review of the docs confirmed the first trap and the composer's buffers against three's
source, the draw count, and the scene claims, and corrected: the dpr 1 explanation (the resolve AO
reads was already paid for; the extra cost is GTAO's copy and blend into a multisampled buffer); the
"today" row (it had mixed plan 21's before and after builds); the ratios (mixed bases, and the half-
resolution row changed the samples too); the missing consequence of the buffer parity; which class
the second trap belongs to; and two stale passages in the study doc (P1 waiting for exercise 8, P5's
premise), now annotated.
